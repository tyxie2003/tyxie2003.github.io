> P: How is it going with your master's life? 
> X: Good, I've learned things. Like you need to have more communication with others. 
> P: Yes, like learning from others around. 
> X: Yes. I don't like to use English in my daily life, because I live in a different culture. However, in academia I think it's necessary to have more conversations with others. 
> P: You take your undergraduate in China. That should make sense that you need time to adapt.
> X: Yeah. I thought I knew how to use English properly. However, yesterday I tried to record myself speaking English, and I found even for myself it's hard to understand. 
> P: I guess you just always speak fast. 
> X: Yes, I tried to speak fast, but I don't have the ability to do that. I think speaking is like singing, you have to control the muscles of your mouth. 

This happened at this noon except I polished it by deleting a lot of pauses and grammar mistakes. I guess you can already see the level of my oral English. 

Apparently there’s objective difficulties. I speak Chinese rather than French, so there’s no way I can draw an analogy or have similar words. Also, I spent 22 years living in a different culture. I am not willing and don’t see it possible to completely fit in another one. That means I won’t have enough exposure to English environment, since I always get along with my Chinese friends.

However, communication is so important in academia. What can you even learn without enough communications? That force you to improve the way you speak to assure efficiency. 

I thought it could be naturally overcome anyway. However, look at the professors, there’s still confusing accents after that long time. At least that means something is hard to change. Those are rocks that wouldn’t be penetrated by water drops. An intended effort seems to be important.

I had a plan for practicing it. First, speak to myself about things I want to express and record it. It can help me knowing what to say when facing others, and also inspect rooted mistakes so that it will no longer cause confusion. Maybe also show it to others to help correct. Second, watching English drama. A whole Breaking Bad series is waiting for me. I will try shadowing their lines while watching, until I can predict what they want to say. 

Nevertheless, look at the positive side. The professors, they do communicate efficiently. Maybe a bit accent won’t harm at all, except for those new international students listeners. However, that cannot exclude the possibility that they did spend effort reconstructing the grammar, or even correct their accent just adequate for others to understand. 

---

Hope I will finish the steps and set my thoughts free in an English conversation.
